Yogurt & Cultured

Ryazhenka

A cultured milk from Eastern Europe made from baked milk: milk held at low heat for hours until creamy and caramel-tinted, then fermented. It tastes like a mild, toasted yogurt.

CategoryYogurt & Cultured
Maker / OriginEastern Europe
StorageKeep sealed in its bottle or jar in the fridge. Stir before drinking.
Shelf lifeAbout 1 week refrigerated; homemade ryazhenka is best within about 4 days.
Best usesDrinking, breakfast, and in baking where a gentle tang is wanted.
SubstitutesKefir mixed with a little baked-milk flavor is hard to fake; use plain yogurt thinned with milk for cooking.
PairingsBerries, honey, dark bread, and baked apples.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Ryazhenka gets its flavor before culturing: the milk is baked low and slow until its sugars brown lightly, a peasant technique from the Russian oven. It is traditionally soured with a spoonful of smetana rather than commercial cultures.

Storing it

Ryazhenka does not freeze well. Make or buy it in small quantities and keep it cold.

Using it

Drink ryazhenka cold as a mild, creamy cultured milk, or use it in pancake and dough batters in place of kefir.

Swaps & pairings

Plain kefir gives a similar drink without the baked note. A mild drinkable yogurt covers breakfast uses.
