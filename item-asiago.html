Asiago

An Italian cow's-milk cheese from the Asiago plateau in Veneto. Young Asiago is smooth and mild; aged Asiago is firm, nutty, and made for grating.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginVeneto, Italy
StorageWrap in wax paper, then loosely in plastic; keep in the cheese drawer.
Shelf lifeAbout 3 to 4 weeks for young Asiago once cut; aged wedges keep 4 to 6 weeks.
Best usesSandwiches, panini, salads, grated over pasta, soups, and risotto.
SubstitutesYoung: Fontina or mild Provolone. Aged: Parmigiano-Reggiano.
PairingsPinot Grigio, apples, cured meats, and focaccia.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Asiago is protected by law and sold at three ages: fresco, pressed and aged about a month, is pale and elastic; mezzano ages 4 to 6 months; vecchio ages a year or more into a hard, granular grating cheese with a sweet, sharp flavor.

Storing it

Rewrap after each use. Aged Asiago tolerates freezing when grated, up to 2 months; young Asiago does not freeze well.

Using it

Slice young Asiago for sandwiches and melts; it softens smoothly. Grate aged Asiago wherever Parmesan works, for a sweeter, nuttier finish.

Swaps & pairings

Fontina matches young Asiago's melt and mildness. For aged wedges, Parmesan and Pecorino Romano cover the grating role.
