Yogurt & Cultured

Every yogurt in every form, traditional, Greek, skyr, and drinkable, plus kefir, sour cream, crème fraîche, and cottage cheese. The complete collection is cataloged here.

Greek Yogurt

Yogurt strained to remove most of its whey, making it thick enough to stand a spoon in, with a dense, tangy taste. It is a strained yogurt, not a separate culture.

Yogurt & Cultured · Greece

Skyr

An Icelandic strained dairy product made from skim milk, thicker and denser than Greek yogurt, with a very mild tang. It is eaten like yogurt but made like a fresh cheese.

Yogurt & Cultured · Iceland

Plain Yogurt

Milk fermented with yogurt cultures into a smooth, tangy gel, sold without added flavors or sweeteners. It comes in regular, low-fat, and nonfat versions.

Yogurt & Cultured · United States

Fruit Yogurt

Yogurt sweetened and flavored, most often with fruit preparations, vanilla, or honey. It is the sweet, ready-to-eat form of yogurt sold in single cups and tubes.

Yogurt & Cultured · United States

Drinkable Yogurt

Yogurt made thin enough to drink, sold in bottles, usually flavored and sweetened. It is yogurt in beverage form, with the same cultures.

Yogurt & Cultured · United States

Kefir

A fermented milk drink made with kefir grains, a culture of bacteria and yeasts. It is thinner than yogurt, tangier, and slightly effervescent.

Yogurt & Cultured · Eastern Europe

Cottage Cheese

A fresh cheese of loose curds dressed with cream and salt, never pressed or aged. It is mild, lumpy, and eaten with a spoon, sweet or savory.

Yogurt & Cultured · United States

Ricotta

An Italian fresh cheese traditionally made by recooking whey until its proteins form soft, fine curds. It is white, mild, and slightly sweet, with a fluffy texture.

Yogurt & Cultured · Southern Italy

Mascarpone

An Italian cream cheese from Lombardy, made by curdling cream with acid. It is ivory, dense, and buttery with a very mild, sweet flavor.

Yogurt & Cultured · Lombardy, Italy

Quark

A fresh German cheese of soured milk, strained to a thick, smooth paste. It is mild and slightly tart, sold at fat levels from skim to cream-enriched.

Yogurt & Cultured · Germany

Fromage Blanc

A fresh French cheese of curdled milk drained to a soft, spoonable consistency, similar to quark. It is mild, fresh, and slightly tart, often sold in tubs like yogurt.

Yogurt & Cultured · France

Labneh

A Levantine strained yogurt, salted and drained until it reaches a soft cheese consistency. It is tangy, creamy, and spreadable, often rolled into balls stored in olive oil.

Yogurt & Cultured · Lebanon

Ayran

A Turkish savory yogurt drink of yogurt, water, and salt, whisked to a light froth. It is thin, refreshing, and unsweetened, served cold with grilled food.

Yogurt & Cultured · Turkey

Lassi

An Indian yogurt drink blended with water or milk, made sweet, often with mango or rose, or salted and spiced. It is frothy, cooling, and served with meals.

Yogurt & Cultured · India

Filmjölk

A Swedish cultured milk, mesophilic and mild, with a distinctive pourable, slightly ropy texture. It is eaten like a thin yogurt at breakfast.

Yogurt & Cultured · Northern Europe

Balkan Yogurt

A set-style yogurt fermented undisturbed in its container, giving a firm, clean-cut gel and a pronounced tartness. It is made with Balkan cultures, typically without thickeners.

Yogurt & Cultured · Balkans

Ryazhenka

A cultured milk from Eastern Europe made from baked milk: milk held at low heat for hours until creamy and caramel-tinted, then fermented. It tastes like a mild, toasted yogurt.

Yogurt & Cultured · Eastern Europe

Smetana

The sour cream of Central and Eastern Europe, cultured cream with a clean tang and a thick, spoonable body, usually around 20 to 30 percent fat in commercial versions.

Yogurt & Cultured · Eastern Europe

Sour Cream

Cream cultured until it thickens and turns pleasantly tart. It is a spoonable, glossy condiment and ingredient, standard in American and Mexican-American cooking.

Yogurt & Cultured · United States

Crème Fraîche

A French cultured cream, thick and velvety with a mild, nutty tang, around 30 to 45 percent fat. It is more stable in cooking than sour cream and less tart.

Yogurt & Cultured · France
