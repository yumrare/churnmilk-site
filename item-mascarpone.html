Mascarpone

An Italian cream cheese from Lombardy, made by curdling cream with acid. It is ivory, dense, and buttery with a very mild, sweet flavor.

CategoryYogurt & Cultured
Maker / OriginLombardy, Italy
StorageKeep sealed in its tub in the fridge. It picks up odors readily, so keep it away from strong foods.
Shelf lifeAbout 1 to 2 weeks; check the date on the tub. Use within a few days of opening for best quality.
Best usesTiramisu, cheesecakes, frosting, pasta sauces, and spooned over fruit.
SubstitutesCream cheese blended with heavy cream, or crème fraîche for a tangier result.
PairingsEspresso, cocoa, berries, figs, and ladyfingers.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Mascarpone is not fermented; it is cream coagulated with citric or tartaric acid and drained, closer to a set cream than a cheese. Its butterfat runs around 44 percent, which is why it melts into sauces like a solid cream.

Storing it

Mascarpone does not freeze well; it separates. If it looks oily, it has gotten warm; chill it and stir gently.

Using it

Fold mascarpone into tiramisu cream and cheesecake batters, or stir a spoonful into hot pasta and risotto at the end for richness.

Swaps & pairings

Cream cheese beaten with a few spoonfuls of cream approximates it in tiramisu. Crème fraîche swaps in where a slight tang is welcome.
