Butter & Spreads

Brown Butter

Butter cooked until the milk solids toast and turn nutty: the French beurre noisette. Not a product but a preparation, made in minutes from any butter.

CategoryButter & Spreads
Maker / OriginFrance
StoragePour it, solids and all, into a heatproof jar. Refrigerate or freeze in portions; re-melt gently.
Shelf lifeUse at once for the fullest aroma; refrigerated in a sealed jar it keeps about 2 weeks, frozen up to 3 months.
Best usesPasta and gnocchi sauces, roasted vegetables, cookies, cakes, and frostings.
SubstitutesPlain melted butter (milder), or ghee cooked a shade longer for a toastier note.
PairingsGnocchi and sage, roasted vegetables, chocolate chip cookies, and blondies.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Brown butter is butter cooked until its milk solids toast to a deep golden brown, the French beurre noisette, named for its hazelnut color and aroma. The water cooks off and the flavor turns nutty, warm, and caramel-like.

Storing it

Brown butter keeps like butter once cooled, but its toasted flavor fades with time, so small batches made fresh are best. Freeze it in an ice cube tray for ready portions to drop into pans and batters.

Using it

Melt butter in a light-colored pan over medium heat, swirling, until it foams, crackles, and smells nutty; pull it off at amber, not brown-black. It is ready the moment the solids look like toasted hazelnuts.

Swaps & pairings

Plain melted butter swaps in anywhere, with a quieter flavor. For a closer match without cooking butter yourself, melt ghee until its remaining solids deepen; the result is similar though less complex.
