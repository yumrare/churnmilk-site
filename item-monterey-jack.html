Monterey Jack

A semi-hard American cheese from Monterey County, California. It is pale, mild, and buttery with small holes, made to melt; aged Dry Jack is its firmer variant.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageWrap in wax paper, then loosely in plastic; keep in the cheese drawer.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 to 3 weeks once opened. It dries at the edges; keep it wrapped.
Best usesQuesadillas, nachos, burgers, enchiladas, grilled cheese, and casseroles.
SubstitutesYoung Gouda, Havarti, or Muenster.
PairingsGreen chiles, lager, apples, and tortilla dishes.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Monterey Jack is named for David Jack, the Monterey merchant who commercialized it in the 1800s, though its roots are in the Spanish missions' queso. Dry Jack, aged longer with a rubbed rind, is the firm, nutty original's grown-up form.

Storing it

Keep sealed away from strong odors. Monterey Jack freezes acceptably when shredded, up to 2 months.

Using it

Monterey Jack is a melting cheese first: shred it into quesadillas, nachos, and sauces where it goes smooth without oiliness. Slice it for sandwiches.

Swaps & pairings

Young Gouda melts the same with more sweetness. Havarti is softer and butterier. Muenster is the closest deli-counter alternative.
