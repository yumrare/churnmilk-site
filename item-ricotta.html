Yogurt & Cultured

Ricotta

An Italian fresh cheese traditionally made by recooking whey until its proteins form soft, fine curds. It is white, mild, and slightly sweet, with a fluffy texture.

CategoryYogurt & Cultured
Maker / OriginSouthern Italy
StorageKeep sealed in its tub in the fridge. If liquid pools, it can be stirred back or drained for a firmer cheese.
Shelf lifeAbout 1 to 2 weeks; fresh ricotta from a deli keeps about 5 days. It sours rather than ages.
Best usesLasagna, stuffed pasta, cannoli, cheesecakes, toast, and pancakes.
SubstitutesCottage cheese blended smooth, or mascarpone for a richer filling.
PairingsHoney, lemon zest, tomatoes, basil, and toast.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Traditional ricotta is made by reheating the whey drained from mozzarella or provolone until the albumin proteins rise as fine curds; the name means recooked. Most American ricotta is made from whole milk with a little whey, richer and more standardized.

Storing it

Ricotta freezes acceptably for cooked dishes for up to 2 months; thawed ricotta is too watery for spreading.

Using it

Drain ricotta in a sieve if a filling must be firm. Whip it with lemon zest and honey for toast, or fold it into pasta at the end of cooking.

Swaps & pairings

Blended cottage cheese is the classic stand-in for fillings. Mascarpone gives a richer, denser result in desserts and pasta.
