Cheese

Burrata

A fresh Italian cheese from Puglia: a pouch of mozzarella filled with stracciatella, shreds of mozzarella soaked in cream. It is eaten days after it is made.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginSouthern Italy
StorageKeep in its liquid in the coldest part of the fridge. It is highly perishable.
Shelf lifeBest within 1 to 2 days of purchase, and by the date on the package. Do not keep opened burrata beyond a day.
Best usesCaprese salads, torn over pizza and pasta, bruschetta, and with grilled peaches or tomatoes.
SubstitutesFresh mozzarella, with a spoonful of ricotta or cream added.
PairingsTomatoes, basil, olive oil, prosciutto, and crusty bread.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Burrata was created in Andria, Puglia, in the early 1900s as a way to use mozzarella trimmings. The outer shell is stretched curd; inside, the stracciatella stays loose and rich. Cut open, the center spills out like thick cream.

Storing it

Never freeze burrata; ice crystals destroy the creamy center. Keep it submerged in its liquid until serving.

Using it

Serve burrata whole at cool room temperature and cut it at the table. Season simply with olive oil, salt, and pepper so the cream is the flavor.

Swaps & pairings

Fresh mozzarella gives the same mild, milky base without the liquid center. A ball of mozzarella with ricotta spooned over imitates the effect.
