Cheese

Colby

An American cheese from Wisconsin, made like cheddar but with a washed curd. It is softer, moister, and milder than cheddar, with an open, springy texture.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageWrap tightly in plastic or wax paper then plastic; keep in the cheese drawer.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 to 3 weeks once opened. It dries faster than cheddar; keep it well wrapped.
Best usesSandwiches, snacking, burgers, cheese boards, and mac and cheese blends.
SubstitutesMild cheddar, Monterey Jack, or Colby-Jack blends.
PairingsApples, crackers, lager, and deli meats.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Colby was created in 1885 in Colby, Wisconsin, by Joseph Steinwand, who adapted the cheddar process with a washed curd that keeps it softer and sweeter. Federal standards even set its moisture higher than cheddar's. Longhorn is the same cheese in a half-moon shape.

Storing it

Keep away from strong odors. Colby freezes acceptably when shredded, up to 2 months, though it turns crumbly.

Using it

Colby is a snacking and sandwich cheese: slice it thick and serve at room temperature. Blend it with cheddar in mac and cheese for a milder sauce.

Swaps & pairings

Mild cheddar is the closest flavor with more firmness. Monterey Jack melts smoother. The two marbled together are sold as Colby Jack.
