Cheese

Fontina

A semi-soft Italian cheese from the Aosta Valley, made from raw cow's milk. It has a washed reddish rind, small holes, and a rich, earthy flavor.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginAosta Valley, Italy
StorageWrap in wax paper or parchment, then loosely in plastic; store in the cheese drawer.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 to 3 weeks once cut. The cut face dries quickly, so keep it covered.
Best usesFonduta (the Piedmontese fondue), polenta, risotto, panini, and gratins.
SubstitutesGruyère, young Gouda, or Emmental.
PairingsNebbiolo, mushrooms, truffles, and roasted potatoes.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

True Fontina Val d'Aosta is protected and made from a single milking of Valdaostana cows. Wheels age about 3 months in mountain caves and tunnels, where they are brushed and salted. Fontina-style cheeses made elsewhere are milder and softer.

Storing it

Keep away from strong odors. Fontina freezes acceptably when grated for cooking, up to 2 months.

Using it

Fontina melts into an exceptionally smooth, stringy sauce, the base of fonduta served over polenta. Remove the rind before melting it into dishes.

Swaps & pairings

Gruyère gives the same silky melt with alpine nuttiness. Young Gouda is milder and widely available. Emmental works in gratins and fondue blends.
