Cheese

Gouda

A Dutch cow's-milk cheese named for the city of Gouda. It ranges from young, mild, and buttery to aged wheels with caramel notes and crunchy crystals.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginNetherlands
StorageWrap in wax paper or parchment and refrigerate; keep the wax rind on an uncut wedge.
Shelf lifeAbout 3 to 4 weeks once cut. Unopened wheels keep for months; aged Gouda keeps longest.
Best usesSandwiches, cheese boards, mac and cheese, soups, and snacking.
SubstitutesEdam, mild Cheddar, or Havarti.
PairingsDark bread, apples, grapes, mustard, brown ale, and riesling.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Gouda curds are washed with warm water before pressing, which rinses away some lactose and gives the cheese its gentle sweetness. Young Gouda is smooth and springy; after a year or more it hardens, deepens to amber, and tastes of butterscotch.

Storing it

Cut away any mold with a clean margin; Gouda is firm enough for this to be safe. Wrap a fresh face of the cheese after cutting so it does not dry out, and store it in the fridge's cheese drawer.

Using it

Young Gouda melts evenly and suits grilled cheese, fondue, and sauces. Aged Gouda is a table cheese: break it into chunks rather than slicing, and let its crystals and caramel flavor stand on their own.

Swaps & pairings

Edam is the natural swap, from the same Dutch tradition, slightly firmer and leaner. Mild Cheddar covers Gouda in sandwiches and sauces, and Havarti matches young Gouda's creaminess in melting dishes.
