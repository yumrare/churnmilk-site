Butter & Spreads

Neufchâtel

A fresh, soft cheese in the cream cheese family, made with more milk and less cream, so it has about a third less fat. It is smooth, mild, and slightly grainier than cream cheese.

CategoryButter & Spreads
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageKeep the opened brick wrapped in its foil inside a sealed container in the fridge.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 weeks once opened; check the date on the package. Fresh cheeses perish faster than aged ones.
Best usesSpreading on bagels, frostings, dips, and lighter cheesecakes.
SubstitutesCream cheese, which is richer and firmer.
PairingsBagels, crackers, fruit, and cheesecake.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

The name comes from Neufchâtel in Normandy, one of France's oldest cheeses, where the original is a bloomy-rind cheese. The American version took the name for a fresh, low-fat cream cheese style and is sold in foil bricks beside the cream cheese.

Storing it

Do not freeze; fresh cheeses turn watery and grainy. Keep it away from strong-smelling foods.

Using it

Use Neufchâtel anywhere cream cheese goes when you want less fat: spreads, frostings, and dips. Soften it at room temperature before beating.

Swaps & pairings

Cream cheese swaps in one for one with a richer result. Light cream cheese is essentially the same product under another name.
