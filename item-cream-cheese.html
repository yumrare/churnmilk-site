Cream Cheese

A soft, fresh, mildly tangy American cheese, spreadable straight from the package. The bagel's partner, and the base of cheesecake and cream cheese frosting.

CategoryButter & Spreads
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageKeep refrigerated in its foil or tub, tightly rewrapped. Bring to room temperature before spreading or baking.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 weeks once opened; unopened foil blocks keep about a month past their date stamp in the fridge.
Best usesBagels, cheesecake, frosting, dips, stuffed peppers, and creamy sauces.
SubstitutesNeufchâtel (lighter), mascarpone, or blended Greek yogurt for spreads.
PairingsBagels and smoked salmon, graham crackers, carrot cake, and fresh berries.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Cream cheese was developed in New York in the 1870s, when dairyman William Lawrence set out to make a richer version of French Neufchâtel and landed on something new. The Philadelphia brand, launched soon after, made it America's bagel standard.

Storing it

Cream cheese molds faster than hard cheeses; discard the block if mold appears rather than trimming it. It freezes acceptably for baking and dips, though the texture turns slightly grainy for spreading.

Using it

Room-temperature cream cheese blends smooth; cold cream cheese lumps. Beat it first in frostings and cheesecakes, soften blocks for dips, and fold it into hot pasta sauces for instant creaminess.

Swaps & pairings

Neufchâtel is the nearest swap and a little lighter. Mascarpone is richer and sweeter, lovely in desserts. For spreads and dips, strained Greek yogurt brings the same tang with a lighter body.
