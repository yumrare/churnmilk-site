Milk & Cream

Clotted Cream

A thick English cream made by slowly heating cream until it clots into a dense, golden-crusted layer. It has butterfat around 55 percent and a cooked, nutty flavor.

CategoryMilk & Cream
Maker / OriginTorquay, England
StorageKeep sealed in the fridge. Serve the golden crust with the cream beneath it, not skimmed off.
Shelf lifeAbout 1 to 2 weeks in the fridge; check the date on the jar. It is a fresh cream and perishes accordingly.
Best usesCream teas with scones and jam, spooned over desserts and fruit.
SubstitutesMascarpone loosened with cream, or very thick whipped cream for scones.
PairingsScones, jam, fresh berries, and strong tea.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Clotted cream is made by baking cream until a golden crust forms over a thick, spoonable layer. In Devon it goes on the scone first, then jam; in Cornwall, jam first. The two counties have argued the order for generations.

Storing it

Clotted cream does not freeze well; its texture turns grainy. Keep it cold and finish the jar within days of opening.

Using it

Serve clotted cream at cool room temperature so it spreads over warm scones. A little goes a long way; it is nearly as rich as butter.

Swaps & pairings

Mascarpone gives the same dense, rich spoonful. Whipped heavy cream is lighter and sweeter in effect. Neither has the baked crust flavor.
