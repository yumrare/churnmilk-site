Soft Serve

A frozen dairy dessert served directly from a machine that freezes and aerates the mix at once. It is lighter, airier, and warmer than hard ice cream, sold in cones and cups.

CategoryIce Cream & Frozen
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageEat soft serve at the shop. At-home pints should be kept deeply frozen and sealed.
Shelf lifeSoft serve is served fresh and does not keep at home; pints labeled soft serve keep about 1 to 2 months in a home freezer before turning icy.
Best usesCones, sundaes, Blizzards and concrete-style blends, and dipped cones.
SubstitutesWhipped frozen yogurt or gelato for the closest at-home textures.
PairingsCones, sprinkles, dipped cones, and sundaes.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Soft serve's signature curl comes from machines that aerate the mix as it freezes, pushing overrun past 50 percent, and the mix itself is lower in fat than hard ice cream. Dairy Queen's 1940 invention turned it into America's roadside dessert.

Storing it

Home pints will never match machine texture; they set harder and icier. Eat them within the month for the closest experience.

Using it

Eat soft serve immediately, before the curl slumps. At home, let soft-serve pints temper longer than ice cream for a closer texture.

Swaps & pairings

Whipped or soft-scoop frozen desserts approximate the airy texture. Regular ice cream is denser but the same flavors.
