Ice Cream & Frozen

Rocky Road

Chocolate ice cream with marshmallow and nuts, born in Oakland, California in 1929. One of the first ice cream flavors with mix-ins.

CategoryIce Cream & Frozen
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageKeep sealed at 0 degrees Fahrenheit or colder; serve after a brief stand so the marshmallow softens.
Shelf lifeBest within 1 to 2 months at 0 degrees Fahrenheit; marshmallow pieces firm up and nuts soften over time.
Best usesSundaes, cones, ice cream cakes, and loaded shakes.
SubstitutesHeavenly hash, tin roof sundae flavors, or chocolate ice cream with toppings added at serving.
PairingsHot fudge, whipped cream, and extra toasted nuts.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Rocky road was created in Oakland, California in 1929 by William Dreyer, co-founder of Dreyer's Ice Cream, who cut marshmallows with his wife's sewing scissors and folded them into chocolate ice cream with walnuts. It was named for the rocky road of the Depression years.

Storing it

Marshmallow turns hard and nuts go soft if the tub warms and refreezes; keep it deep in the freezer. A rock-hard tub is a temperature problem, not spoilage; let it stand before scooping.

Using it

Rocky road was the original loaded ice cream: chocolate, chew, and crunch in every bite, and the template for every mix-in flavor since. It needs no building; serve it in a cone or under hot fudge as it is.

Swaps & pairings

Heavenly hash is the nearest relative, built on chocolate with marshmallow, nuts, and chocolate pieces. Moose tracks-style flavors swap the marshmallow for peanut butter cups and fudge. Plain chocolate plus fresh toppings rebuilds it at serving.
