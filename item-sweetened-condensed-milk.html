Milk & Cream

Sweetened Condensed Milk

Milk concentrated like evaporated milk but with sugar added, making a thick, sweet syrup. It is canned and shelf-stable until opened.

CategoryMilk & Cream
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageStore cans in a cool, dry pantry. Transfer leftovers to a sealed jar in the fridge after opening.
Shelf lifeUnopened cans keep about a year in the pantry. Once opened, refrigerate and use within about 2 weeks.
Best usesKey lime pie, magic bars, fudge, tres leches, Thai and Vietnamese iced coffee.
SubstitutesEvaporated milk plus sugar for baking, adjusted to taste.
PairingsKey lime pie, Vietnamese coffee, tres leches, and dulce de leche.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Condensed milk sweetened a pre-refrigeration world: Gail Borden patented it in 1856, and Civil War orders made it famous. The sugar preserves it, and its syrupy body builds candies and pies that fresh milk cannot without long cooking.

Storing it

It thickens further when cold. Do not freeze it in the can. A can simmered unopened becomes dulce de leche, a traditional use.

Using it

Use sweetened condensed milk as both milk and sugar in recipes. In coffee drinks it is stirred in hot or poured over ice.

Swaps & pairings

Evaporated milk with about a cup of sugar per can approximates it in recipes. Coconut condensed milk is the common non-dairy version.
