Ice Cream & Frozen

Sorbet

A frozen dessert of sweetened fruit juice or purée and water, with no dairy. It is dense, intensely fruity, and icy-smooth when churned well.

CategoryIce Cream & Frozen
Maker / OriginFrance
StorageKeep sealed in the back of the freezer, with plastic wrap on the surface.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 to 3 months in the freezer unopened; about 1 month once opened. It ices over faster than dairy desserts.
Best usesPalate cleansers between courses, dairy-free desserts, champagne floats, and light finishes to rich meals.
SubstitutesSherbet for a touch of dairy richness, or granita for a coarser Italian ice.
PairingsChampagne (for palate cleansing), mint, and fresh fruit.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Sorbet's name comes through Arabic and Turkish serbet, the cooled fruit drinks of the Ottoman court, though the frozen dessert took its modern form in Italian and French kitchens. It contains no dairy at all, which is why it is the fruit flavor in its purest frozen form.

Storing it

Serve sorbet slightly colder than ice cream; it softens into syrup rather than cream. Do not refreeze melted portions.

Using it

Serve a small scoop of sorbet between courses as a palate cleanser, or pair it with champagne. Let it temper only briefly; it melts faster than ice cream.

Swaps & pairings

Sherbet is the dairy-adjacent swap. Granita gives the same fruit ice in a grainy, spoon-scraped form.
