Cheese

Cotija

An aged Mexican cheese from Michoacán, made from cow's milk. It is firm, dry, and very salty, grated or crumbled over dishes the way Parmesan is used in Italy.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginMexico
StorageWrap in wax paper, then loosely in plastic; keep in the cheese drawer.
Shelf lifeAbout 3 to 4 weeks once cut; it is a hard cheese and keeps well. Surface mold can be trimmed away.
Best usesElote (Mexican street corn), tacos, enchiladas, beans, salads, and soups.
SubstitutesParmigiano-Reggiano, Pecorino Romano, or ricotta salata.
PairingsElote, beans, lime, chili powder, and grilled corn.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Cotija is named for the town of Cotija in Michoacán. Wheels age in caves and warehouses through the rainy season, growing firm and intensely salty. Because it is dry and hard, it grates into a powder that coats food rather than melting into it.

Storing it

Cotija dries further with age and gets saltier; grate it sooner for the mildest flavor. Grated cotija freezes up to 2 months.

Using it

Grate cotija fine with a rasp grater and shower it over hot dishes at the table. Use a lighter hand than with Parmesan; it is saltier.

Swaps & pairings

Parmigiano-Reggiano is the classic stand-in, milder and nuttier. Pecorino Romano is closer in salt. Ricotta salata crumbles similarly but gentler.
