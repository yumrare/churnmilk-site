Cheese

Gorgonzola

An Italian blue cheese from Lombardy, made from cow's milk in two main styles: dolce, soft and sweet, and piccante, firm and sharp. Both are veined with blue mold.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginLombardy, Italy
StorageWrap in foil and keep in the fridge's cheese drawer, away from other foods.
Shelf lifeAbout 1 to 2 weeks for dolce once opened; piccante keeps about 3 to 4 weeks.
Best usesRisotto, gnocchi sauce, pizza, salads with walnuts and pears, and cheese boards.
SubstitutesStilton, Roquefort, or Danish blue.
PairingsHoney, walnuts, pears, Barolo, and sweet wines.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Gorgonzola is named for a town near Milan and protected by law. Wheels are pierced with needles so air reaches the interior and the blue veining develops. Dolce is aged about 2 months and spoonable; piccante ages longer and slices.

Storing it

Blue cheese keeps giving off moisture; rewrap in fresh foil every few days. Freezing dulls it, but crumbled Gorgonzola freezes for cooking.

Using it

Stir dolce into hot risotto or pasta at the end for an instant sauce. Serve piccante in wedges with honey and nuts on a board.

Swaps & pairings

Stilton gives a firmer, buttery blue. Roquefort is sharper and sheep-based. For a milder blue, use dolce where piccante is called for.
