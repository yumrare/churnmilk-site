Cheese

Gruyère

A firm Swiss alpine cheese from the Fribourg region, aged 5 to 12 months or more. Its dense paste turns from smooth and nutty to firm and crystalline as it ages.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginFribourg, Switzerland
StorageWrap in wax paper or parchment, then loosely in plastic; keep in the cheese or vegetable drawer.
Shelf lifeAbout 3 to 4 weeks once cut. An unopened wedge keeps for months in the fridge.
Best usesFondue, French onion soup, gratins, quiche, croque monsieur, and cheese boards.
SubstitutesComté, Emmental, or Jarlsberg.
PairingsDry white wines, lager, cornichons, cured ham, and dark bread.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Gruyère is named for the town of Gruyères and protected under Swiss law. Wheels are brushed and turned for months in aging cellars, developing a brown rind and a flavor of toasted nuts, fruit, and brown butter.

Storing it

Keep the cut face covered and rewrap after each use. Gruyère freezes acceptably when grated, up to 2 months, for cooking.

Using it

Gruyère is the classic melting cheese of fondue and onion soup. Grate it for even melting, and serve aged wedges at room temperature on a board.

Swaps & pairings

Comté is the closest match, nuttier and French. Emmental brings the same alpine melt with bigger holes, and Jarlsberg gives a milder, sweeter result.
