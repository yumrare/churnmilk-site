Ice Cream & Frozen

Sherbet

A frozen dessert of fruit juice or purée with a little milk, between sorbet and ice cream in richness. It is icy, bright, and usually citrus or berry flavored.

CategoryIce Cream & Frozen
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageKeep sealed in the back of the freezer. Press plastic wrap onto the surface after serving.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 to 3 months in the freezer unopened; about 1 month once opened. Its low fat means ice crystals form faster.
Best usesFloats, punches, rainbow servings, and light desserts.
SubstitutesSorbet for a dairy-free version, or light ice cream for a richer one.
PairingsGinger ale floats, lemon-lime soda, and shortbread.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Sherbet occupies the legal middle ground: enough milk to be more than sorbet (1 to 2 percent), not enough to be ice cream. Its fruit-forward tartness and light body made it the bridge dessert of the mid-20th century, from rainbow sherbet to the classic orange punch-bowl staple.

Storing it

Eat opened sherbet within the month; it ices over sooner than ice cream. Do not refreeze melted portions.

Using it

Scoop sherbet into ginger ale or lemon-lime soda for a float, or float it in fruit punch. Serve it as the light counterpoint to rich ice cream scoops.

Swaps & pairings

Sorbet swaps directly for dairy-free guests. A light citrus ice cream gives the same refreshment with more richness.
