Butter & Spreads

Beurre Blanc

A French butter sauce of wine, vinegar, and shallots reduced and whisked with cold butter. It is a sauce rather than a spread, but butter is its substance.

CategoryButter & Spreads
Maker / OriginFrance
StorageServe at once, or hold in a warm (not hot) spot or a thermos. Heat breaks the emulsion.
Shelf lifeIt does not keep as a sauce; hold it warm for up to about 1 hour. Leftover sauce can be chilled and rewarmed gently once.
Best usesSauce for fish, shellfish, chicken, and vegetables.
SubstitutesLemon butter melted gently, or hollandaise for a richer sauce.
PairingsPoached fish, salmon, scallops, and asparagus.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Beurre blanc was created near Nantes in the early 1900s, by most accounts when a chef's béarnaise broke and she served the butter and shallot reduction anyway. The Muscadet wine of the region is traditional in the reduction.

Storing it

If it breaks, whisk in a spoonful of cream or cold water off the heat. Never boil it; the butter will separate into oil.

Using it

Reduce wine, vinegar, and shallot, then whisk in cold butter a few cubes at a time off the boil until glossy. Serve immediately over fish.

Swaps & pairings

Hollandaise is the richer cousin, built on yolks. Melted lemon butter gives the same flavors without the emulsion work.
