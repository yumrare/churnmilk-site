Cheese

Edam

A semi-hard Dutch cheese named for the town of Edam, usually sold coated in red wax. It is mild, slightly salty, and nutty, made from partly skimmed milk.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginNetherlands
StorageKeep the wax on the uncut side; wrap the cut face in wax paper, then loosely in plastic.
Shelf lifeAbout 3 to 4 weeks once cut; unopened waxed wheels keep for months.
Best usesSandwiches, cheese boards, salads, gratins, and baked dishes.
SubstitutesGouda, especially young Gouda, or Jarlsberg.
PairingsStone fruit, melons, lager, and dark bread.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Edam is traditionally coated in red paraffin wax, which let it travel the world on Dutch ships for centuries. Inside, the paste is pale gold and firm with a few small holes. Most Edam is sold young; aged versions turn harder and sharper.

Storing it

Rewrap the cut face after each use. Edam freezes acceptably when grated, up to 2 months, for cooking.

Using it

Slice Edam thin for sandwiches and boards; it holds its shape and cuts cleanly. Young Edam melts evenly into gratins and baked pasta.

Swaps & pairings

Young Gouda is the nearest Dutch neighbor, a little richer. Jarlsberg gives a similar firm slice with a sweeter note. Babybel-style cheese is Edam in miniature.
