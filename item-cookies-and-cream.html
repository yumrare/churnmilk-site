Ice Cream & Frozen

Cookies & Cream

Vanilla ice cream loaded with crushed chocolate sandwich cookie pieces. An American invention that rose to fame in the early 1980s and never left the top flavors list.

CategoryIce Cream & Frozen
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageKeep sealed at 0 degrees Fahrenheit or colder. The cookie pieces stay firmest in a consistently cold freezer.
Shelf lifeBest within 1 to 2 months at 0 degrees Fahrenheit; the cookie pieces soften further the longer it stores.
Best usesSundaes, shakes, ice cream sandwiches, and pie crust desserts.
SubstitutesCookies and cream frozen yogurt, or vanilla ice cream with cookies crushed over the top.
PairingsHot fudge, whipped cream, and extra crushed cookies.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Cookies and cream is generally credited to American makers around 1980, when crushed chocolate sandwich cookies were first folded into vanilla ice cream at scale. Within a few years it had become one of the country's most popular flavors.

Storing it

The cookie chunks are the first thing to suffer from temperature swings, turning completely soft. Keep the tub deep in the freezer and reseal it promptly after scooping.

Using it

Cookies and cream is a complete dessert in a scoop: base, mix-in, and crunch in one. It builds thick shakes, fills ice cream sandwiches, and needs nothing more than hot fudge to become a sundae.

Swaps & pairings

For an exact match there is no real substitute; the flavor is the cookie. Cookies and cream frozen yogurt is the lighter swap, and vanilla ice cream with fresh-crushed cookies on top restores the crunch the stored kind loses.
