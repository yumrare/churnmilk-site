Cheese

Camembert

A soft French cheese from Normandy with a white bloomy rind and a creamy, yielding center. It is sold in small rounds and ripens from the rind inward.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginNormandy, France
StorageKeep in its box or wrapped in wax paper in the fridge; let it breathe rather than sealing it airtight.
Shelf lifeAbout 1 to 2 weeks. Eat within a few days of its ripest point; an ammonia smell means it is past.
Best usesCheese boards, baked whole in its box, spread on baguette, and melted into sauces.
SubstitutesBrie is the closest match in flavor and format.
PairingsChampagne, cider, baguette, apple slices, and fig jam.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Camembert dates to the late 1700s in the village of Camembert, Normandy. Under its mold-ripened rind the paste softens as it ages, turning buttery with notes of mushroom and cream. It is ripe when it gives gently under the thumb.

Storing it

Store away from strong odors, which the rind absorbs. Do not freeze; the texture turns grainy and wet.

Using it

Bring Camembert to room temperature for at least an hour before serving. Bake a whole round until molten for dipping bread and vegetables.

Swaps & pairings

Brie swaps in almost one for one, slightly milder and made in larger wheels. A ripe Camembert-style cheese from another region is the next best.
