Ice Cream & Frozen

Cookie Dough

Vanilla ice cream filled with chunks of chocolate chip cookie dough, usually with chocolate chips of its own. An American flavor popularized in 1984.

CategoryIce Cream & Frozen
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageKeep sealed at 0 degrees Fahrenheit or colder; let the tub stand a few minutes so the dough chunks soften slightly before serving.
Shelf lifeBest within 1 to 2 months at 0 degrees Fahrenheit; the dough chunks gradually firm up in deep cold.
Best usesSundaes, shakes, ice cream cakes, and straight from the tub.
SubstitutesVanilla ice cream with fresh cookie pieces, or cookie dough frozen yogurt.
PairingsCaramel sauce, warm cookies, and chocolate milkshakes.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Cookie dough ice cream took off in 1984, when Ben & Jerry's added chunks of chocolate chip cookie dough to vanilla ice cream after a customer suggested it on a shop suggestion board. The dough is made without raw egg risk, using heat-treated flour.

Storing it

Dough chunks turn hard if the freezer runs very cold; a short stand at room temperature fixes the texture. Keep the tub in the back of the freezer and the lid tight against freezer burn.

Using it

Cookie dough is a texture flavor: the chew of the dough against the smooth base is the whole appeal. It anchors ice cream cakes and over-the-top sundaes, and it is rarely improved by more than caramel sauce.

Swaps & pairings

There is no clean swap for the dough chunks themselves. Vanilla ice cream served with a warm cookie on the side covers the same ground with better texture. Cookie dough frozen yogurt is the lighter stand-in.
