Yogurt & Cultured

Skyr

An Icelandic strained dairy product made from skim milk, thicker and denser than Greek yogurt, with a very mild tang. It is eaten like yogurt but made like a fresh cheese.

CategoryYogurt & Cultured
Maker / OriginIceland
StorageKeep sealed in its cup in the fridge. Stir any surface whey back in.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 to 3 weeks unopened; about 1 week after opening. Check the date on the cup.
Best usesBreakfast, smoothies, dips, baking, and as a dessert base with berries.
SubstitutesGreek yogurt, ideally a thick 2% or whole-milk style.
PairingsBerries, honey, granola, and brown sugar.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Skyr has been made in Iceland since the Viking settlement, kept alive by passing cultures from farm to farm. Technically it is a strained fresh cheese eaten like yogurt. Traditional skyr uses skim milk, which keeps it lean despite its density.

Storing it

Skyr freezes acceptably for up to 2 months for smoothies and cooking; the texture turns grainy for eating plain.

Using it

Eat skyr as a thick breakfast yogurt with berries and honey, or use it in dips and baking where Greek yogurt is called for.

Swaps & pairings

Greek yogurt is the direct swap, slightly tangier and looser. Quark gives a similar dense, mild result in European recipes.
