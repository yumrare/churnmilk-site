Mochi Ice Cream

Bite-sized balls of ice cream wrapped in sweet rice dough (mochi). Each piece is one or two bites: chewy outside, frozen inside.

CategoryIce Cream & Frozen
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageKeep sealed in its tray in the freezer, with the film on the pieces until serving.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 to 3 months in the freezer unopened; about 1 month once opened. The mochi dough dries out once exposed.
Best usesSnacking, party trays, and bento-style dessert boxes.
SubstitutesIce cream bonbons, or dango-style sweets for the chewy outside alone.
PairingsGreen tea, mango, chocolate, and matcha.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Mochi ice cream was invented in the 1990s by a Japanese-American company that wrapped bite-sized ice cream in sweet rice dough. The dough must stay soft when frozen, which took years of recipe work, and it goes gummy fast if it sits out.

Storing it

Serve each piece right from the freezer; the dough turns gummy as it warms. Do not refreeze thawed pieces.

Using it

Serve mochi ice cream straight from the freezer, one piece at a time, so the dough stays chewy and the center stays firm.

Swaps & pairings

Chocolate-dipped ice cream bites give a similar one-bite format. Dango or mochi sweets cover the chewy side without the ice cream.
