Mint Chocolate Chip

Cool mint ice cream studded with chocolate pieces, often tinted pale green. Created in England in 1973 and now a global standard.

CategoryIce Cream & Frozen
Maker / OriginTorquay, England
StorageKeep sealed at 0 degrees Fahrenheit or colder. Mint absorbs and gives off aromas easily, so keep the lid tight.
Shelf lifeBest within 1 to 2 months at 0 degrees Fahrenheit; the mint flavor fades noticeably with long storage.
Best usesSundaes, shakes, brownie desserts, and holiday menus.
SubstitutesMint chocolate chip gelato, or chocolate ice cream with mint syrup.
PairingsChocolate brownies, hot fudge, and chocolate liqueur.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Mint chocolate chip was created in 1973 by Marilyn Ricketts, a culinary student in Torquay, England, who entered it in a competition to create an ice cream for the wedding of Princess Anne. It went on to become a worldwide flavor.

Storing it

If the green has paled and the mint tastes flat, the tub is past its best. Store it away from strong-smelling frozen foods, and press parchment onto the surface to limit ice crystals.

Using it

Mint chocolate chip is the refresher among rich flavors: it resets the palate after chocolate-heavy desserts and turns a warm brownie into a complete plate. It also makes a famously green milkshake.

Swaps & pairings

Mint chocolate chip gelato is the denser swap and is often made with white, untinted base. Peppermint ice cream, a seasonal relative, is sharper and usually carries candy pieces instead of chocolate.
