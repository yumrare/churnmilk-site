Milk & Cream

Buttermilk

A thick, tangy cultured milk, traditionally the liquid left from butter churning and now made by culturing low-fat milk. It pours like thin cream and tastes mildly sour.

CategoryMilk & Cream
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageRefrigerate in its sealed carton. Shake before use, as it separates slightly.
Shelf lifeAbout 2 to 3 weeks unopened; about 1 to 2 weeks after opening. It thickens and tangs further with age.
Best usesBiscuits, pancakes, cornbread, fried chicken marinades, and ranch dressing.
SubstitutesMilk plus lemon juice or vinegar (a tablespoon per cup), or thinned plain yogurt.
PairingsFried chicken, cornbread, biscuits, and ranch dressing.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Real cultured buttermilk is the liquid left from churning cultured cream into butter; nearly all sold today is cultured skim or low-fat milk, tangled with cultures until it thickens. Its acid tenderizes baked goods and reacts with baking soda to lift them.

Storing it

Buttermilk freezes well for up to 3 months; thaw and whisk. Its tang is a feature, but discard it if it smells putrid rather than sour or turns pink or blue.

Using it

Use buttermilk where its acid works for you: tenderizing chicken in a marinade, lifting biscuits and pancakes with baking soda, and sharpening dressings.

Swaps & pairings

A cup of milk soured with lemon juice covers baking. Plain yogurt thinned with milk matches the body for marinades and dressings.
