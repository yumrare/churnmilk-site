Butter & Spreads

Compound Butter

Softened butter beaten with herbs, aromatics, or sweeteners, then chilled in a log or tub. A slice melts over hot food as an instant sauce.

CategoryButter & Spreads
Maker / OriginFrance
StorageRoll in parchment or plastic into a log and refrigerate; slice off coins as needed.
Shelf lifeAbout 1 to 2 weeks in the fridge with fresh herbs; up to 3 months frozen.
Best usesFinishing steaks and fish, vegetables, corn, breads, and baked potatoes.
SubstitutesA drizzle of herb oil, or plain butter plus fresh herbs sprinkled on top.
PairingsSteak, grilled fish, corn on the cob, and warm bread.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Compound butter is an old restaurant technique: a log of flavored butter waits in the fridge, and a coin of it melts over a finished dish as the sauce. French kitchens call the classic version maître d'hôtel butter, with parsley and lemon.

Storing it

Freeze the log and slice from frozen; the flavorings keep well. Keep it wrapped tightly to avoid absorbing odors.

Using it

Soften butter, beat in the flavorings, roll and chill. Cut a coin and lay it on hot steak, fish, or vegetables to melt as it is served.

Swaps & pairings

Herb oil gives the same flavors without dairy richness. Garlic butter is one specific compound; the category covers any blend you like.
