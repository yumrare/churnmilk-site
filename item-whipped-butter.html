Butter & Spreads

Whipped Butter

Butter whipped with air until light and fluffy, sold in tubs. It spreads straight from the fridge and melts fast over hot food.

CategoryButter & Spreads
Maker / OriginUnited States
StorageKeep the tub sealed in the fridge; return it promptly after serving so it stays fluffy.
Shelf lifeAbout 1 to 2 months in the fridge. Do not freeze; the whipped texture breaks down.
Best usesSpreading on toast and rolls, topping pancakes, corn, and baked potatoes.
SubstitutesSoftened stick butter, or a butter spread blend.
PairingsToast, muffins, warm rolls, and baked potatoes.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Whipped butter is butter beaten with air, and sometimes a little milk, until it is light and fluffy. The air makes it spread straight from the fridge and feel lighter per spoonful, though a cup of it holds less butter than a cup of stick butter.

Storing it

Keep crumbs out of the tub and always use a clean knife; whipped butter's airy structure shows every contamination. If it weeps liquid or smells stale, it has turned.

Using it

Whipped butter is a table butter, made for spreading and melting over hot food. Its lightness is the point: it will not measure or bake like stick butter, and it melts too fast for sautéing.

Swaps & pairings

For spreading, softened stick butter is the same thing without the air. In recipes measured by weight they swap evenly; by volume they do not, so avoid whipped butter in baking unless the recipe calls for it.
