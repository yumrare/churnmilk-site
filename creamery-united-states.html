America built its dairy case on standards: graded butter, Philadelphia-style cream cheese, and the ice cream parlor flavors that spread worldwide in the twentieth century.

Salted Butter

Churned cream with salt added, at about 80 percent butterfat. Salted butter is the classic table butter: spreadable, seasoned, and the longest-keeping everyday butter.

Butter & Spreads · United States

Unsalted Butter

Churned cream with no salt added: the baker's standard butter. Pure, sweet cream flavor, and full control of a recipe's seasoning.

Butter & Spreads · United States

Whipped Butter

Butter whipped with air until light and fluffy, sold in tubs. It spreads straight from the fridge and melts fast over hot food.

Cream Cheese

A soft, fresh, mildly tangy American cheese, spreadable straight from the package. The bagel's partner, and the base of cheesecake and cream cheese frosting.

Vanilla

The classic ice cream flavor, made with real vanilla or vanilla extract. Vanilla is consistently among the best-selling ice cream flavors in the United States.

Cookies & Cream

Vanilla ice cream loaded with crushed chocolate sandwich cookie pieces. An American invention that rose to fame in the early 1980s and never left the top flavors list.

Cookie Dough

Vanilla ice cream filled with chunks of chocolate chip cookie dough, usually with chocolate chips of its own. An American flavor popularized in 1984.
