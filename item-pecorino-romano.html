Pecorino Romano

A hard, salty Italian grating cheese made from sheep's milk, produced mainly in Sardinia and Lazio. It is aged at least 5 months and often 8 or more.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginSardinia, Italy
StorageWrap tightly in foil or plastic, or in wax paper then plastic; keep in the cheese drawer.
Shelf lifeAbout 4 to 6 weeks once cut; whole wedges keep for months. It dries rather than spoils.
Best usesCacio e pepe, carbonara, amatriciana, grated over beans, soups, and vegetables.
SubstitutesParmigiano-Reggiano for a milder result, or aged Asiago.
PairingsFull-bodied red wines, honey, and ripe pears on a board.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Pecorino Romano is one of Italy's oldest cheeses, made since Roman times and once a ration for soldiers. Wheels are salted repeatedly as they age, giving a firm, crumbly paste and a sharp, peppery bite stronger than Parmesan's.

Storing it

If the surface dries or molds, trim it away; the cheese beneath keeps well. Grated Pecorino freezes for up to 2 months.

Using it

Grate Pecorino fine and add it to hot pasta with some of the cooking water to form a sauce. Use a lighter hand than with Parmesan; it is saltier.

Swaps & pairings

Parmigiano-Reggiano is the classic substitute, sweeter and less salty. An aged Manchego wedge grates similarly for pasta in a pinch.
