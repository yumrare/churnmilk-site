Melting Cheese Guide

Cheeses ranked by how they melt, from the great stretchers to the ones that only soften. Melt depends on moisture, age, and acid: young, moist cheeses melt smoothly, and aged cheeses need gentler handling.

RankCheeseHow it melts
1MozzarellaThe great stretcher. Low-moisture mozzarella melts into long, even strands; fresh mozzarella melts softer and wetter.
2ProvoloneMelts smooth and drapes cleanly, with more flavor than mozzarella. A sandwich and baked-pasta staple.
3GoudaYoung Gouda melts evenly into sauces and grilled cheese. Aged Gouda melts poorly; serve it as a table cheese.
4Swiss (Emmental)Melts into smooth strands and browns well, the fondue and French onion soup classic.
5CheddarMelts well when grated and heated gently; high heat or long cooking can turn it grainy and oily.
6BrieThe rind holds it back, but trimmed Brie melts into a creamy pool. Bake the whole wheel and it slumps.
7ParmesanDoes not stretch. It dissolves into sauces and risottos when grated fine and added off the heat.
8FetaSoftens and turns creamy but keeps its shape. It bakes rather than melts, which is its strength.
