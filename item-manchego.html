Manchego

A firm Spanish cheese from La Mancha, made only from the milk of Manchega sheep. Its rind carries a woven zigzag pattern from the molds it is pressed in.

CategoryCheese
Maker / OriginLa Mancha, Spain
StorageWrap in wax paper, then loosely in plastic; keep in the cheese drawer. A light coat of olive oil on the cut face is traditional.
Shelf lifeAbout 3 to 4 weeks once cut; aged wedges keep longer than young ones.
Best usesTapas, cheese boards, grated over beans and stews, and shaved into salads.
SubstitutesPecorino Romano for aged wedges, or a firm aged Gouda.
PairingsMembrillo (quince paste), Marcona almonds, Rioja, and dry sherry.
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Manchego is protected by law and sold at set ages: fresco, semicurado, curado, and viejo, from weeks to a year or more. The paste is dense and oily with small holes, tasting of sheep's milk, hay, and toasted nuts as it ages.

Storing it

Keep away from strong-smelling foods. Manchego freezes acceptably when grated, up to 2 months, for cooking.

Using it

Serve Manchego in thin triangular slices with the rind as a handle, at room temperature. Curado and viejo grate well over hot dishes.

Swaps & pairings

For aged Manchego, Pecorino Romano gives the same firm, salty grate. Asiago and aged Gouda cover the board role with a milder profile.
